# ADR-0009: Stage handoff carries a structured rationale brief; three-layer enforcement

## Status
Accepted — 2026-05-12

## Context

Blueprint v2 specified append-only artifacts with bidirectional supersession links (ADR-0005). The mechanism preserves decision history. But it does not address the practical problem at handoff time: a downstream sub-agent receiving an upstream artifact needs to know WHY decisions were made, not just WHAT was decided.

Research round 2 surfaced three converging findings:

1. **External memory should be the source of truth for durable facts; the compaction summary is a navigation tool, not a memory store.** (Claim C-R2-0017, Zylos April 2026.)
2. **Structured compaction templates outperform freeform summarization;** required sections act as checklists preventing silent information loss. (Claim C-R2-0016, Zylos April 2026, Factory.ai benchmark differentiator.)
3. **79% of multi-agent failures originate from specification and coordination issues rather than model capability.** (Claim C-R2-0025, Cemri et al. via MAST.)

Cross-stage rationale loss is one of the failure modes Q-B (ii) names: downstream stages forget *why* a resolution was chosen and contradict it. This is MAST FM-2.6 (reasoning-action mismatch, 13.2%) and FM-2.5 (ignoring other agents' input, 1.9%) when it manifests through unstructured handoff.

The existing pipeline practice — pass file paths between stages — is necessary but not sufficient. Sub-agents do read the artifacts they're handed, but ADR rationale is buried inside artifact files, often in sections sub-agents skim past while focusing on their task content. A focused, structured brief at the top of every invocation makes the rationale prominent.

This ADR commits to the brief and specifies how it is enforced.

## Decision

**Every sub-agent invocation in the feature-pipeline starts with a structured rationale brief.** The brief is generated by the orchestrator at stage-transition time and prepended to the sub-agent's invocation prompt as its first content section.

### Brief structure (required template, no freeform)

```markdown
## Rationale Brief — Stage <N>: <stage-name>

### Session Intent
<1-3 sentences: the user's original intent, slugged feature, current run-id>

### Decisions Inherited (immutable for this stage)
- ADR-<id>: <one-line summary> [version <semver>]
- ADR-<id>: <one-line summary> [version <semver>]
- ...

### Decisions Added in Prior Stages (this run)
- Stage 5 introduced ADR-<id>: <one-line summary>
- Stage 6 issue-resolution superseded ADR-<id> v<v1> with v<v2>: <reason>
- ...

### Active Constraints (manifest hard constraints + carried-forward soft constraints)
- <constraint>: <one-line>
- ...

### Open Items From Upstream (handed off to your stage)
- Item: <description>. Expected handling: <action this stage should take>
- ...

### Previously-Resolved Issues (verified or dismissed; do not re-surface)
- Issue I-<id>: <one-line summary>. Resolution: <brief>. State: verified at iteration <N>.
- ...

### Files You Should Read (in order)
1. <path> — <reason>
2. <path> — <reason>
...
```

The template is mandatory. Freeform briefs are explicitly disallowed; they regress to the failure mode this discipline exists to prevent.

### Brief size budget

The brief targets **500-1500 tokens** per invocation. Larger briefs erode the sub-agent's working context budget (per claim C-R2-0011, working budget is 20-30% of effective context, ~25-40K tokens; the brief at 1500 tokens is 4-6% of that).

Brief content rules:
- Each ADR entry is one line summary. Full ADR is referenced by ID; sub-agent reads the full file only if needed.
- "Previously-resolved issues" section lists at most 10 entries (the most recently resolved). Older verified issues are accessible via `issues-ledger.json` but not surfaced in the brief by default — surfacing only the most recent prevents brief size from growing unboundedly across iterations.
- If the run has many active ADRs (>15), the brief lists the most relevant 10 for this stage and includes a count-of-others-available.

The orchestrator owns the brief generation logic. Knowledge skills do not duplicate it.

## Three-layer enforcement

A brief is only useful if the receiving sub-agent uses it. Three enforcement layers compose:

### Layer 1 — Orchestrator includes the brief in every sub-agent invocation

The orchestrator skill (per ADR-0001) is responsible for:
- Reading the relevant ADRs and prior-stage outputs from disk
- Generating the structured brief per the template above
- Passing it as the **first content section** of the Agent tool invocation prompt

This is enforcement by construction: the brief is *present* in every sub-agent's context. The sub-agent cannot operate without seeing it (it's at the top of the prompt).

The orchestrator-side check: a pre-invocation validation step verifies the brief is non-empty, has all required sections, and is under the size budget. If validation fails, the orchestrator regenerates rather than dispatching.

### Layer 2 — Knowledge skills instruct sub-agents to honor the brief

Every knowledge skill that loads into a sub-agent (per ADR-0010 frontmatter pattern) includes a section:

```
## Honoring the Rationale Brief

Before producing your output, read the Rationale Brief at the top of your invocation prompt. Your output MUST NOT contradict any decision listed in `## Decisions Inherited` or `## Decisions Added in Prior Stages`.

If you believe a prior decision should be changed:
- Do NOT silently change it.
- Emit a `proposed_supersession` entry in your output with:
  - The ADR being challenged (by id and version)
  - The reason the prior decision should be revisited
  - The proposed new direction
- The next critique stage will surface this to human triage.

If you find an `Open Item From Upstream` you cannot handle in your stage, mark it explicitly as `deferred_to_<future-stage>` in your output rather than ignoring it.

Previously-resolved issues listed in the brief MUST NOT be re-opened by your output unless you have evidence of re-emergence. If you have such evidence, mark the relevant issue as `reopen_recommended` with the evidence — the next critique stage will verify.
```

This text is shared across all knowledge skills via the `document-conventions-knowledge` skill (per ADR-0010, that's the always-loaded base skill).

### Layer 3 — Critique verifies the brief was honored

The next critique stage (Critique-1 for early-pipeline outputs; Critique-2 for late-pipeline outputs) includes brief-honor checks in its critique-knowledge skill:

```
## Brief-Honor Verification

When reviewing an artifact produced by an upstream sub-agent, verify:

1. **Decision contradiction check.** Does the artifact contradict any decision in the upstream agent's rationale brief? Cross-reference each architectural commitment in the artifact against the brief's `Decisions Inherited` and `Decisions Added in Prior Stages` sections. If a contradiction exists without a corresponding `proposed_supersession` entry, that is a HIGH severity issue (category: `adr_contradiction`).

2. **Open-item handling check.** Did the upstream agent address every `Open Item From Upstream` in the brief? Each open item must be either resolved in the output OR marked explicitly as `deferred_to_<future-stage>`. Silent dropping is a MODERATE severity issue (category: `dropped_open_item`).

3. **Re-surfaced verified issue check.** Did the upstream agent re-open any issue listed in the brief's `Previously-Resolved Issues` section without `reopen_recommended` evidence? If so, MODERATE severity issue (category: `step_repetition`).
```

This is the backstop. When Layers 1 and 2 fail (the agent has the brief, was told to honor it, but didn't), Layer 3 catches the failure as a critique issue that flows into the standard issue lifecycle.

## Why three layers, not one

Single-layer enforcement fails predictably:

- **Orchestrator alone (Layer 1):** the brief is *present* but the sub-agent has no instruction to consult it. Sub-agent reads the task content, ignores the brief, produces inconsistent output. MAST FM-2.5 (ignoring other agents' input).
- **Knowledge skill alone (Layer 2):** the instruction is *present* but the brief is not, so there's nothing to consult. Sub-agent follows the rule vacuously.
- **Critique alone (Layer 3):** failures are caught after they happen, costing an additional iteration each time.

Three layers compose:
- Layer 1 guarantees the data is available.
- Layer 2 instructs use.
- Layer 3 catches violations and feeds them into the issue lifecycle.

None alone is sufficient. All three together provide defense-in-depth.

## Concrete example

Stage transition: synth-designer (Stage 5) → synth-critic-1 (Stage 6).

After synth-designer writes `05-blueprint-v1.md`, the orchestrator does the following before invoking synth-critic-1:

1. Read ADRs from `working/feature/<feature-slug>/04-synthesis/adrs/` and `working/feature/<feature-slug>/adrs/` (the synthesis-stage ADRs plus any added by Designer).
2. Read `issues-ledger.json` and extract issues with `current_state in {verified, dismissed}` whose `origin.affected_artifacts` references the blueprint or its predecessors.
3. Generate the brief:

```markdown
## Rationale Brief — Stage 6: Critique-1

### Session Intent
User intent: "Add a /healthz endpoint with database connectivity check." Feature-slug: `add-healthz-endpoint`. Run-id: `add-healthz-endpoint-20260512-093000`.

### Decisions Inherited (immutable for this stage)
- ADR-0001: Orchestrator is a Skill at main session (not a sub-agent) [v1.0.0]
- ADR-0002: Critique-1 uses single-critic CoVe (asymmetric debate reserved for Critique-2) [v1.0.0]
- ADR-0005: Append-only artifacts with bidirectional supersession [v1.0.0]
- ADR-0007: GitNexus primary, codebase-memory-mcp fallback for code-graph queries [v2.0.0]

### Decisions Added in Prior Stages (this run)
- Stage 4 (synthesis) introduced ADR-RUN-0001: Health check returns aggregate status, not per-dependency
- Stage 5 (designer) introduced ADR-RUN-0002: Endpoint sits at /healthz (Kubernetes convention), not /api/health

### Active Constraints
- no_new_runtime_infrastructure (from manifest)
- must_compose_only_claude_code_primitives (from manifest)
- response time budget: < 200ms p99 (from blueprint §4.A13)

### Open Items From Upstream
- Item: Designer noted database connectivity check needs a timeout decision. Expected handling: Critique-1 to surface as an issue if the blueprint doesn't specify one.

### Previously-Resolved Issues
(None — this is the first iteration of Critique-1 for this feature.)

### Files You Should Read (in order)
1. working/feature/add-healthz-endpoint/05-blueprint-v1.md — the artifact under review
2. working/feature/add-healthz-endpoint/04-synthesis/report.md — synthesis grounding (sections referenced by blueprint)
3. working/feature/add-healthz-endpoint/issues-ledger.json — current state (will be empty at this iteration)
```

4. Pass this as the first content section of synth-critic-1's invocation.

When synth-critic-1 runs:
- Layer 1 satisfied: the brief is in its prompt.
- Layer 2 active: the critique-1-knowledge skill instructs it to honor the brief.
- Layer 3 will activate on the *next* critique stage (Critique-2) verifying synth-critic-1's own output for brief-honor.

When synth-critic-1 produces its issues, the orchestrator carries that output AND a freshly-generated brief into Stage 7 (synth-planner). The new brief includes any ADRs synth-critic-1 referenced. And so on through each stage.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- **Defense against MAST FM-2.6 (reasoning-action mismatch, 13.2%)** by making rationale prominent at every invocation.
- **Defense against MAST FM-2.5 (ignoring other agents' input, 1.9%)** by explicit instruction to honor upstream decisions.
- **Defense against MAST FM-1.3 (step repetition, 15.7%) and FM-2.1 (unexpected conversation reset, 2.2%)** by surfacing previously-resolved issues in the brief.
- **Reduces context bloat from full-artifact handoffs.** Per claim C-R2-0027, multi-agent token duplication ranges 53-86% across frameworks; structured brief reduces this. The sub-agent reads referenced files only when needed, not by default.
- **Bridges the per-feature ledger (ADR-0008) into runtime context.** The ledger is the persistent store; the brief is the per-invocation surfacing.
- **Mandatory template prevents silent information loss.** Per claim C-R2-0016, structured templates outperform freeform — this is the explicit application of that finding.

**Negative:**

- **Orchestrator complexity grows.** The brief generator must read multiple sources, deduplicate, summarize, and format. This logic lives in the orchestrator skill (per ADR-0001), which becomes longer. Mitigated by isolating brief-generation into a clear sub-routine.
- **Layer-2 instruction text duplicated across knowledge skills.** Mitigated by placing it in `document-conventions-knowledge` and having every other knowledge skill reference it rather than duplicate. (Per ADR-0010, document-conventions-knowledge is always preloaded.)
- **Layer-3 verification adds critique work.** Each critique stage's checklist grows by three checks (decision contradiction, open-item handling, re-surfaced verified issue). Critics already run on a budget; this adds ~5-10% to per-critique latency. Acceptable.
- **Brief generation is potentially flaky** if the ADR or ledger files are mis-formatted. Mitigated by Layer 1's pre-invocation validation: malformed inputs produce a regeneration retry, then fail loudly if still malformed.

**Neutral:**

- The brief is JSON-serializable-equivalent (markdown with well-defined sections). Could be promoted to JSON format if downstream tooling needs it. v1 stays markdown for human-readability during debugging.

## Alternatives considered

**No brief; rely on artifact references alone.** This is the v2 status quo. Per the failure-mode research (claim C-R2-0024 MAST data), it leaves the pipeline exposed to 13.2% reasoning-action mismatch and adjacent failures. Rejected.

**Single-layer enforcement (orchestrator-only).** Brief present but not instructed. Predictable failure: sub-agent ignores. Rejected.

**Full artifact embedding.** Include every upstream artifact in full in the sub-agent's invocation. Solves rationale availability but blows the context budget — per claim C-R2-0027 this is the source of the 53-86% token duplication. Rejected.

**Brief generated by an LLM at handoff time** (rather than templated from ADRs and ledger). Considered. LLM summarization paradoxically lengthens trajectories by 13-15% (claim C-R2-0014). Templated extraction from structured sources is more reliable. Rejected.

**Brief as a side artifact rather than prompt prepend.** Write brief to disk, point sub-agent at it via file path. Tempting because it keeps the prompt clean. Rejected because Layer 1's "the brief is at the top of the prompt" guarantee is the strongest enforcement — file-path references can be ignored by an agent that doesn't read them.

## Evidence

- C-R2-0011 (context budget allocation 10-15% system, 15-20% tools, 30-40% knowledge, 20-30% working, 10-15% buffer). Brief budget at 500-1500 tokens fits within working budget.
- C-R2-0016 (structured templates outperform freeform). Mandatory template per this ADR.
- C-R2-0017 (external memory as source of truth, summary as navigation). Brief IS the navigation; ADRs and ledger ARE the memory.
- C-R2-0024 (MAST failure mode frequencies). Direct defenses against FM-1.3, FM-2.5, FM-2.6, FM-2.1.
- C-R2-0025 (79% of failures from specification/coordination). Brief addresses coordination failure specifically.
- C-R2-0027 (53-86% token duplication across frameworks). Brief reduces handoff payload.
