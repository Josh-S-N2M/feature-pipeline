# Technology selection methodology — consensus synthesis

## (1) Is a weighted-scoring matrix the right tool?

**Consensus (plain English).** A weighted matrix is the right tool in a specific regime: roughly 3–8 options on 4–8 criteria of differing importance, where you need an auditable rationale. Below that it is overhead; above it, it produces false precision. Its real value is not the number it spits out — it is forcing the argument to shift from "which option do I like" to "what do we actually weight," and making that reasoning visible. It is a decision *aid*, not a decision *machine*. Authoritative sources do not crown one method; they map method to the decision. ATAM (SEI) is the canonical alternative when the goal is *risk discovery* rather than ranking — it surfaces sensitivity and tradeoff points and deliberately avoids precise scoring. Documented failure modes and mitigations are consistent across sources: too many criteria (cap at ~8); equal weighting (the most common failure — force tradeoffs); halo/anchoring (score one criterion across all options, with anchors defined first); false precision (sensitivity-test by shifting the top weight ±5–15%); and weight-gaming (lock criteria and weights *before* seeing option data).

**Settled:** match method to decision; matrix-as-aid; the five failure modes and their mitigations; lock weights before scoring. **Contested:** whether to use pairwise/AHP weighting at all — AHP carries a still-disputed rank-reversal flaw and weights are not portable into a simple weighted sum.

## (2) Reversibility / volatility weighting

**Consensus.** Reversibility is not just another criterion — it is a *process selector and weight multiplier*. Bezos's one-way/two-way-door framing is the settled foundation: irreversible decisions get heavy process; reversible ones get decided fast. Modern practice couples door-type directly to rigor (one-way → full matrix + external review; two-way → quick; trivial → <15 min). For genuinely reversible choices, capability and speed dominate and reversibility's weight is low; for irreversible ones, exit-cost can outrank raw capability. A key 2026 refinement: **reversibility decays** — two-way doors silently become one-way as data, consumers, and teams accumulate, so judge by the reversal-to-implementation cost *ratio* and preserve option value with abstraction layers.

**Settled:** reversibility is first-class and gates process intensity. **Contested:** no agreed *numeric* weight relative to capability/maturity — it is directional, not a fixed percentage.

## (3) Re-evaluation triggers

**Consensus.** The dominant anti-pattern is the trigger that never fires: a standalone date-based ADR has no enforcement, so divergence is silent. Best practice is a concrete "Revisit if" clause naming signals (cost > $X, error rate > Y%, SLA change), plus a calendar backstop. The strongest mechanisms *couple the trigger to the running system*: fitness functions / ArchUnit checks that fail CI, and expiry-triggered tests that pass until a review date then fail — converting time into an active signal. ADRs are immutable: supersede, never edit; record confidence level and reconsideration conditions.

**Settled:** signal-based beats pure date-based; immutability/supersession; embed triggers in the record. **Contested/weakly-sourced:** specific cadences (quarterly 30-min sweep, 12-month tags) — see could-not-verify.

## (4) Build-vs-buy for AI infra

**Consensus.** The old "build for differentiation, buy for commodity" tree misfires because the cost surface moves quarterly. Replace the differentiation axis with **workload-specificity measured against a written eval set** (a number, not a narrative). Empirically: buy the commodity model/gateway/observability layers; build the eval suite, orchestration, memory, and context — the agentic control plane where switching cost accumulates. Wardley mapping and Moore's core-vs-context remain the structural filters; wrap vendors in abstraction layers; re-map every ~2 quarters. Hybrid catches ~60% of decisions.

**Settled:** eval-set-as-axis; control-plane in-house; abstraction layers; re-decide on cadence. **Contested:** observability's disposition (sources internally inconsistent — buy vs build).

## Implications for your rubric

- **Keep the pipeline.** Hard-constraint screen → weighted score on survivors is validated: filter by dominant/hard criteria first, score only survivors, cap at ~8 criteria.
- **Anchors:** one-sentence definition + per-level scale for each criterion; word every criterion so high = desirable (prevents scale-reversal); score one criterion across all options.
- **Weights:** make **reversibility/exit-cost** a first-class criterion that *also* multiplies process intensity; lock weights before scoring; run a ±5–15% sensitivity stress test and treat sub-10% gaps as "too close to call."
- **Anchor scoring to evidence,** not feature lists — for AI infra, score against a written eval set.
- **Record:** confidence level, the reversal path, and a concrete signal-based "Revisit if" clause backed by CI enforcement (fitness function or expiry test), not a bare date.
